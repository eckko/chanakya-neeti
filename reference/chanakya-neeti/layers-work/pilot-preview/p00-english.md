00.01.1a | Having bowed with my head to Viṣṇu, the Lord, master of the three worlds, I shall declare a compendium of royal policy drawn from many treatises.
00.01.1b | ~
00.01.2a | Having studied this according to the rule, the best of men knows what is to be done and what is not to be done, the good and the bad, as made known by the teaching of dharma.
00.01.2b | ~
00.01.3a | Therefore, out of desire for the welfare of people, I shall declare it fully, that by the mere understanding of which one attains the state of knowing all.
00.01.3b | ~
00.01.4a | By instructing a foolish pupil, by maintaining a wicked woman, and by association with the miserable, even a learned man comes to grief.
00.01.4b | ~
00.01.5a | A wicked wife, a deceitful friend, a servant who answers back, and dwelling in a house with a snake in it: this is death itself, there is no doubt.
00.01.5b | ~
00.01.6a | One should guard wealth against adversity, and guard one's wife even by means of wealth; but one should always guard oneself, even by means of wife and even by means of wealth.
00.01.6b | ~
00.01.7a | One should guard wealth against adversity. Whence can adversity come to the wealthy? Fortune sometimes moves away, and even what has been hoarded perishes.
00.01.7b | ~
00.01.8a | In a country where there is no respect, no livelihood, no kinsmen, and not even any acquisition of learning, one should not make one's dwelling.
00.01.8b | ~
00.01.9a | A rich man, a Veda-learned brahmin, a king, a river, and fifth, a physician: where these five are not found, one should not stay there even for a day.
00.01.9b | ~
00.01.10a | Means of livelihood, fear, modesty, courtesy, and a generous disposition: where these five are not found, one should not settle there.
00.01.10b | ~
00.01.11a | One should test servants when sending them on an errand, kinsmen when calamity comes, a friend in times of misfortune, and a wife when wealth is lost.
00.01.11b | ~
00.01.12a | He who stands by one in illness, when calamity has come, in famine, in danger from an enemy, at the king's gate and at the cremation ground, he is a kinsman.
00.01.12b | ~
00.01.13a | He who, abandoning what is certain, pursues the uncertain: his certain things perish, and the uncertain is indeed lost.
00.01.13b | ~
00.01.14a | A wise man should choose a maiden born of a good family even if she is ugly, but not a beautiful one belonging to a low-born man; marriage is within a family of equal standing.
00.01.14b | ~
00.01.15a | One should never place trust in rivers, in those with weapons in hand, in clawed and horned beasts, nor in women and royal households.
00.01.15b | ~
00.01.16a | Nectar should be taken even from poison, gold even from filth, good conduct even from an enemy, and a well-spoken saying even from a child.
00.01.16b | ~
00.01.17a | Women's food is twofold, their modesty fourfold, their boldness sixfold, and their desire is declared to be eightfold.
00.01.17b | ~
00.02.1a | Falsehood, rashness, deceit, foolishness, excessive greed, impurity and cruelty are the faults of women, born of their nature.
00.02.1b | ~
00.02.2a | Food, the power to eat it, the power of love, a beautiful woman, wealth and the power to give: this is not the fruit of a small austerity.
00.02.2b | ~
00.02.3a | He whose son is obedient, whose wife follows his wishes, and who is content with his wealth: for him heaven is indeed right here.
00.02.3b | ~
00.02.4a | Those are sons who are devoted to their father; he is a father who nourishes; that is a friend in whom there is trust; she is a wife in whom there is contentment.
00.02.4b | ~
00.02.5a | One should shun such a friend who ruins one's work behind one's back and speaks sweetly to one's face, a pot of poison with milk at its mouth.
00.02.5b | ~
00.02.6a | One should not trust a bad friend, nor should one trust even a friend; a friend, once angered, might reveal every secret.
00.02.6b | ~
00.02.7a | One should not at all disclose by speech a task planned in the mind; one should keep it hidden by deliberation and also set it into action.
00.02.7b | ~
00.02.8a | Foolishness is indeed painful, and youth too is indeed painful; and living in another's house is more painful than painful.
00.02.8b | ~
00.02.9a | A ruby is not on every mountain, nor a pearl in every elephant; good men are indeed not everywhere, nor is sandalwood in every forest.
00.02.9b | ~
00.02.10a | The wise should constantly engage their sons in various good conducts; those who know policy and are endowed with good conduct become honoured in their family.
00.02.10b | ~
00.02.11a | That mother is an enemy and that father a foe by whom the children were not taught; they do not shine in the midst of an assembly, as a crane among swans.
00.02.11b | ~
00.02.12a | From indulgence come many faults, in chastisement many merits; therefore one should chastise one's son and pupil, not indulge them.
00.02.12b | ~
00.02.13a | With a verse, or half of it, or even a syllable of a quarter of it, and with acts of giving and study, one should make the day not barren.
00.02.13b | ~
00.02.14a | Separation from a beloved wife, insult from one's own people, the remainder of a debt, service to a bad king, and a friend who turns away because of one's poverty: these five burn the body without fire.

00.02.15b | ~
00.02.16a | Learning is the strength of brahmins, the army likewise the strength of kings, wealth the strength of vaiśyas, and service that of śūdras.
00.02.16b | ~
00.02.17a | A courtesan abandons a penniless man, the subjects a broken king, birds a tree whose fruit is gone, and a guest, having eaten, the house.
00.02.17b | ~

00.02.18b | ~
00.02.19a | One of bad conduct, of evil gaze, dwelling in a bad place, a wicked man: when men make friendship with such a one, a man quickly perishes.
